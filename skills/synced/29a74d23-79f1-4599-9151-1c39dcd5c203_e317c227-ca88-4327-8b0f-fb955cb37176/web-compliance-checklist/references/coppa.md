# COPPA (Children's Online Privacy)

## When this applies

Any website/app that is directed at children under 13, OR has actual knowledge it's collecting personal info from a user under 13 — regardless of stated target audience. The "directed to children" test considers actual marketing materials, user reviews, and the age composition of similar products' userbases, not just the operator's intent.

**Don't take the stated target audience at face value.** A product nominally aimed at teens/students can still trigger COPPA if its real userbase skews younger (a study tool, a school-adjacent utility, a game). Ask directly whether any plausible real users are under 13.

## Current status — this is active, not future

The FTC finalized amendments to the COPPA rule in 2025; the compliance deadline was April 22, 2026. As of any date after that, enforcement is live, not a "check back later" item. Amendments expanded "personal information" to include biometric identifiers, added written information-security-program and data-retention-policy requirements, and require separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's data to third parties for purposes not integral to the core service (e.g., targeted advertising).

## What compliance requires

- Verifiable parental consent before collecting personal info from a known/likely under-13 user
- A privacy notice specifically covering children's data practices
- A written data retention policy (can't keep children's data indefinitely)
- A written information security program

This is a materially heavier lift than standard privacy compliance — if there's real reason to think under-13 users are present, this needs dedicated attention, not a bullet point in the general privacy policy.

## Ask before writing

- Who realistically uses this (not just who it's marketed to)?
- Any age-gating currently in place, and is it effective or just a checkbox?
